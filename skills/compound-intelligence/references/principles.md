# The book’s three pillars

**Source:** Caroline Webb, *Leadership Intelligence* (2026), Introduction, pp. 1–7; the three core-science sections, pp. 10–13, 116–119, 226–229. Framework labels are the author’s; this overview is a paraphrase.

## Deliberate Wisdom — Making Good Choices

**Widen** perspective and **Lighten** cognitive load. The book describes attention and information processing as selective and limited. Seek relevant perspectives, examine assumptions, and give complicated thinking an external structure. Chapters 1–7 move from understanding and prioritizing through ideas, decisions, meeting preparation, disagreement, and team discussion.

Do not convert “widen” into endless research or “lighten” into ignoring inconvenient complexity. A simple structure should make uncertainty visible, not hide it.

## Intrinsic Inspiration — Motivating Your People

Support **Competence** and **Connection**. Help people see how they can make a meaningful contribution, exercise genuine agency, and work with others toward something worthwhile. Chapters 8–14 cover goals, change, communication, delegation, feedback, development, and collaboration.

Do not reduce motivation to praise, charisma, or persuasive wording. Check the path, resources, rewards, decision space, and relationships. Intrinsic rewards do not excuse unfair compensation or unmanageable work.

## Reasoned Resilience — Rising to the Challenge

Make difficulty more **Manageable** and **Meaningful**. Acknowledge the challenge, stabilize the response, and change what can be changed. Chapters 15–21 cover pressure, upheaval, difficult relationships, struggling colleagues, mistakes, exits, and healthy team resilience.

Do not call denial a reframe or endurance a solution. The team-level guidance includes capacity and recovery, not only individual coping.

## How the book is organized

The 21 challenge chapters generally move through **The Challenge → The Role Model → The Advice**, with additional spotlights. Each part ends with a team-level chapter. This package preserves the challenge and advice distinctions, but does not reproduce the author’s role-model stories as reusable scripts or pretend that the model is those people.

The closing guidance, pp. 333–334, is **Reflect, Repeat, Remind**. This is the source foundation for deliberate practice. The package’s consent rules, learning-record schema, review statuses, and evaluation procedures are implementation additions, not claims made by the book.

Here “intelligence” means practiced leadership judgment and skill. This package does not measure IQ, assign personality types, or treat the three pillars as fixed traits.
