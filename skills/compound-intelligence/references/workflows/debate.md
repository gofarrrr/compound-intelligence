# Bounded deliberation

**Implementation architecture:** adapted from Compound Writing’s debate pattern. Source ideas come from the selected Webb chapter cards; the debate is not an experiment or independent validation.

Begin with a concrete decision tension, not an invitation to generate disagreement for entertainment. Read the relevant evidence, constraints, chapter cards, and any actual panel outputs. If no genuine tension remains, state that and provide the useful synthesis without extra rounds.

## At most four rounds

1. **Positions:** each relevant lens gives its strongest supported recommendation and the trade-off. Use independent workers only when available and authorized; otherwise explicitly label sequential model-generated analysis.
2. **Challenges:** identify the precise conflict and ask what assumption, evidence, or value makes the recommendations differ. Do not let one lens invent facts to win.
3. **Responses:** each lens may hold, concede, or propose a changed action, with a concise reason. A lack of tool response is an unavailable contribution, not a fabricated concession or vote.
4. **Resolution:** distinguish a resolved evidence question, a useful conditional proposal, and an unresolved value choice. Stop if positions repeat or further progress requires new evidence.

A real factual gap is answered by acquiring evidence with permission, not by more simulated debate. A conflict of legitimate values may remain for the user to decide. Keep safety, dignity, and factual integrity as constraints, not bargaining chips.

## Output

Give the strongest alternative positions, what changed through challenge, unresolved uncertainty, the recommended next action or evidence request, and one coaching insight. Do not reproduce pages of agent dialogue or claim a consensus proves the answer correct. Do not create follow-up work, reminders, or durable lessons without the relevant request and capability.
