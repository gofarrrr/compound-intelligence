# Reflect, Repeat, Remind

**Source:** Closing Words, pp. 333–334; decision process versus outcome in Chapter 4; Land the Learning in Chapter 19. The record structure and generalization rules below are implementation design.

## First establish what actually happened

Read the available authorized plan and the user's account. Distinguish the intended action, what was actually tried, the observed or reported result, and what remains unknown. If the attempt has not occurred, help plan a future reflection instead of inventing an outcome.

For a return to a saved case, first follow [resume](resume.md): read its current record, compare the relevant prior position with the new account, and preserve corrections and conflicting reports. Save any update only within the user's actual case permission; do not confuse a return with authorization to write.

**Reflect:** What worked or did not? What evidence supports that? What else might explain the outcome? Did the method fit the situation, and was it applied as intended? Was the decision process reasonable given information available at the time? Do not confuse a lucky outcome with a sound process or an unlucky outcome with incompetence.

**Repeat:** Identify one small, specific adjustment or a reason to keep the current move. Define the kind of situation in which it should be tried again and a signal that would indicate a poor fit. A suggested experiment is not a proven personal rule.

**Remind:** Choose a useful cue or a proposed review occasion. Only create an actual reminder when requested and a scheduling tool succeeds. Otherwise provide a reminder text or suggested date explicitly as unscheduled.

## Useful reflection output

State the attempted move and the result in the user’s terms, one supported observation, one tentative explanation, one adjustment, and what to notice next. Keep the explanation transferable without turning it into a judgment of the user’s character or a colleague’s motives.

A user can decide that a specific preference should be remembered immediately. A case-derived causal lesson should remain a scoped provisional experiment until evidence and the user’s confirmation support broader use. Follow the [save workflow](save.md) for any persistent record. Do not write automatically at the end of every coaching conversation.
