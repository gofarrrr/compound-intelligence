# Rehearse, debrief, retry

**Book foundation:** Triple-A and When-Then, Chapter 5; the selected conversation framework; Show and Explain, Chapter 13. The simulation protocol is implementation design.

Read the relevant chapter card and establish the user’s goal, the facts actually available, and any important boundary. Use the supplied information before asking for more. A single useful difficulty is enough; do not require a full psychological profile of the other person.

## Simulation boundary

Say that the exchange is a plausible practice scenario, not a prediction of how the real person thinks or will respond. If important details are invented for practice, label them explicitly. Do not create false allegations, confidential facts, or stereotypes to make the scenario dramatic. If the real situation is unsafe, use a boundary or support-planning exercise rather than pressuring the user to confront someone.

## Run a small exchange

Let the user try an opening or show one brief demonstration if they ask for a model. Play only the agreed role, with behavior grounded in the scenario rather than an invented diagnosis. Respond in a plausible way that gives the user room to practice. Do not deliberately overwhelm them or reward manipulation.

After a useful exchange, pause. Name one observable move that worked, one place to improve when warranted, and the book principle behind that improvement. Ask for a retry of the specific moment, not the entire conversation. Compare the two attempts against the same criterion.

If the user asks for a complete script rather than live role-play, supply a compact branching scaffold with clear placeholders and label all counterpart responses as hypothetical. Preserve real choices and direct statements; a scripted question must not disguise a fixed decision as consultation.

## Finish

Provide one phrase or action the user can adapt, the underlying lesson, and one signal to notice in the real conversation. A favorable simulated response is not evidence that the real intervention will succeed. No automatically saved transcript, colleague profile, or scheduled follow-up.
