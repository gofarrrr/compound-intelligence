# Review an action before trying it

**Architecture adapted from Compound Writing’s final-pass pattern.** This is a design quality check, not a validated scoring instrument or a named Webb framework.

Read the actual proposed action, current case facts, selected chapter card, and the shared contracts. Do not assess an imaginary plan. If the plan is missing, help form a small one from available context or ask for the material that is genuinely needed.

## Check six things

**Fit:** Does the action address the observed bottleneck, or only the initial label? Is there a nearer chapter or a missing prerequisite?

**Evidence:** Which claims are source guidance, observed facts, reports, and interpretations? Are any examples, motives, scores, dates, promises, or research claims invented? Does the cited chapter support the move?

**Agency and dignity:** Are choices genuine? Are the people involved allowed a perspective? Are boundaries and power differences considered? No coercive or deceptive use of the technique.

**Feasibility and conditions:** Does the user have the authority, time, capacity, resources, and support required? Does the proposal change a structural problem or merely ask someone to endure it?

**Risk and reversibility:** What could go wrong, who bears the downside, and what would stop or modify the attempt? Is qualified human review or a governing process needed?

**Learning:** Can the user explain the selected move? Is there a small practice and an observable check? Are process quality and eventual outcome kept separate?

Use supported findings, not invented scores or an impressionistic “92% ready.” Prioritize the few issues that materially affect the action. A minor wording preference should not outweigh a serious factual or agency problem.

## Check prerequisites of the action

Check what this specific move requires using the evidence already supplied; do not require a managerial title or a permissions interview for every conversation. Preparing a proposal is different from implementing a reassignment.

| Supplied condition | Advice consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Assignment authority, recipient readiness and capacity are reported. | Prepare a scoped handoff with real constraints, support and proposed check-ins; do not ask for these facts again. |
| The user explicitly lacks assignment authority. | Prepare the decision for whoever can approve it, rather than command reassignment. |
| Authority is unknown. | Keep reassignment conditional on approval. Ask only if immediate implementation advice needs the answer now. Unknown does not mean prohibited or approved. |
| Authority exists but the recipient has no free capacity. | Address the scope, timing or resource trade-off; moving the work does not create capacity. |
| The approving owner is unknown. | State the unresolved decision and approval-route gap; identifying that route is a next step, not an invented owner. |

A useful owner handoff contains the reported constraint, decision needed, viable options and their limits, known owner or unresolved approval route, and what the user can prepare now. Use the [support example](../examples/adaptive-coaching.md#decision-handoff-when-reassignment-is-not-ready); “ask the owner” alone is insufficient.

Keep failure types distinct. A false permission statement is an unsupported assertion; an imperative can instead omit a necessary approval check or require unavailable capacity. G and an `advisory_pass` do not replace this manual check. Preparation grants neither the user nor the assistant authority to carry out the action.

## Give a proportionate verdict

**Ready to try:** a bounded action with sufficient basis and no identified critical blocker. State the main limitation and what to observe.

**Conditional:** useful direction, but name the exact condition to satisfy or assumption to check first. Explain how it changes the recommendation.

**Pause for a critical gap:** unsafe proposal, missing consequential fact or authorization, fabricated record, or a decision outside the skill’s role. Say what can still be done safely now and what responsible human process or evidence is needed.

These are recommendations about the plan, not permission to send messages, change systems, hire or fire people, or bypass the user’s judgment. “Ready” does not mean guaranteed effective. Do not force repeated review rounds; revise the critical issue and stop when the requested task is complete.
