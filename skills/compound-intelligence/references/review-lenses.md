# Canonical review lenses

The names and paired principles are from the book. The review prompts and return format are **implementation adaptations**. A lens is a way to examine a plan, not an independent person or source of empirical evidence.

## Deliberate Wisdom — Widen / Lighten

Read the relevant Chapter 1–7 cards, not automatically all seven. Examine the aim, evidence, assumptions, missing perspectives, criteria, options, and cognitive load. Ask whether the apparent simplicity clarifies the problem or conceals uncertainty. Identify the most useful missing observation or a specific decision-process improvement. Do not invent a numerical score or treat a neat table as evidence.

## Intrinsic Inspiration — Competence / Connection

Read the relevant Chapter 8–14 cards. Examine whether the plan supplies a credible path, resources, actual agency, coherent incentives, respectful feedback, or a meaningful shared purpose. Look for false choices, vague praise, persuasion that hides facts, and demands that the conditions do not support. Distinguish helpful challenge from a demand merely relabeled as stretch.

## Reasoned Resilience — Manageable / Meaningful

Read the relevant Chapter 15–21 cards. Examine pressure, uncertainty, dignity, control, recovery, workload, and the effects on other people. Look for denial disguised as reframing and structural problems disguised as individual weakness. Ask what should be changed, stopped, supported, or escalated. Do not diagnose health or make employment decisions.

## Return format

Keep each review short: the strongest supported concern; its evidence or explicit assumption; one proposed improvement; the trade-off it introduces; and what could change the advice. Apply the shared safety and evidence rules under every lens. A majority of lenses cannot overrule a critical factual or safety gap.
