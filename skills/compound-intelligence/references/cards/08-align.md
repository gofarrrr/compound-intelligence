# 08 · How to Get People Aligned on a Goal

**Framework:** Pillars of Possibility — Purpose, Path, People, Progress

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 8, printed pp. 121–135. **Pillar:** Intrinsic Inspiration.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Make a goal meaningful, credible, owned, and trackable so people can align around it.

## Route elsewhere when
Use ci-change when the goal is understood but daily behavior or incentives are not changing. Capacity failures must be addressed, not relabeled motivation failures.

## Coaching explanation
Webb’s motivation framework supports both competence and connection: people need a worthwhile purpose, an achievable route, real contribution, and evidence of progress.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Purpose
Clarify who benefits and what successful achievement would look like. Use a few meaningful themes or a memorable phrase only when they reflect the actual goal.

### Path
Connect the goal to concrete intermediate steps, process and outcome measures, dependencies, and available resources. Distinguish an ambitious route from an unsupported demand.

### People
Give people genuine ownership and room to shape the work. Use Check and Stretch to explore what would have to be true for a bigger aspiration to be feasible. Take constraints seriously.

### Progress
Make meaningful progress visible and agree a useful review rhythm. Recognize specific contributions and use reviews to remove barriers, not merely intensify pressure.

## Useful output
An alignment brief covering purpose, credible path, owners and real choices, progress signals, and unresolved capacity or dependency gaps.

## Original coaching question
Which pillar is weakest: do people doubt the point, the path, their role, or whether progress is real?

## Small practice
Ask one colleague to describe the path and their decision space in their own words; use the difference to improve the plan.

## Observe and adjust
People can identify both their contribution and a feasible next step; enthusiasm alone is not the measure.

## Common misuse
Do not use intrinsic motivation as a substitute for fair pay, realistic resources, or honest discussion of constraints.
