# 04 · How to Make an Important Decision

**Framework:** ACORN — Aim, Criteria, Options, Rating, Next Steps

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 4, printed pp. 58–75. **Pillar:** Deliberate Wisdom.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Evaluate an important choice with available options, competing criteria, uncertainty, or consequences.

## Route elsewhere when
Use ci-understand for an unclear situation and ci-ideate for a lack of options. Employment choices require the human-review boundary in safety.md.

## Coaching explanation
Webb makes judgment more deliberate by externalizing what the decision is for, what matters, and how alternatives compare. A good outcome alone does not establish that the decision process was sound.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Aim
Define the actual outcome and decision owner before defending a favorite option. State timing, reversibility, and what is within the user’s authority.

### Criteria
Agree the relevant criteria and genuine dealbreakers before rating options. Make trade-offs and any weights explicit. Do not derive criteria to justify a predetermined winner.

### Options
Include meaningful alternatives and, where relevant, the status quo. Do not assume only the two options in the initial framing are available.

### Rating
Assess each option against each criterion using supplied evidence. Leave unknowns visible. Use numbers only when a defensible scale and inputs exist; never fabricate a numerical decision matrix. Eliminate true dealbreaker failures rather than compensating for them with unrelated strengths.

### Next Steps
Investigate decisive gaps, test a close call, or use the user’s stated principles to resolve a genuine tie. Revisit criteria transparently when they were incomplete, not to game the result. Check a comfortable winner for motivated reasoning.

## Supporting techniques and spotlights
Future Back examines how a proposed choice could fail; Now Forward explores consequences from the present. Stage, Track, Pivot uses proportionate commitments, useful early signals, and explicit conditions for adapting. Hiring Wisely (pp. 72–73) emphasizes consistent job-relevant criteria, questions, and evidence; this skill may structure the process, not rank people or decide hiring. Leadership Principles (pp. 73–75) can be developed from the user’s own considered experiences; do not invent their values.

## Useful output
A decision brief with aim, criteria and dealbreakers, options, evidence and unknowns, trade-offs, recommendation where appropriate, and a test or next-step plan. Separate process assessment from outcome.

## Original coaching question
What would you need to see to change your preferred option?

## Small practice
Before the next consequential choice, write the criteria before reviewing the options, and record what evidence would make you pivot.

## Observe and adjust
The recommendation follows the declared criteria, and the most consequential uncertainty remains visible.

## Common misuse
Do not launder intuition into arbitrary scores, promise outcomes, or treat a reviewer panel as independent empirical evidence.
