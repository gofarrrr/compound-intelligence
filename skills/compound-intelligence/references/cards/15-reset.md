# 15 · How to Stay Steady and Energized Under Pressure

**Framework:** Five Resets — Recenter, Reframe, Reward, Recharge, Reflect

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 15, printed pp. 231–248. **Pillar:** Reasoned Resilience.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Help the user steady their immediate response to pressure and choose a manageable next action.

## Route elsewhere when
An ongoing team capacity problem needs ci-team-resilience. Threats, serious distress, and unsafe conditions need appropriate support; this is not clinical care.

## Coaching explanation
Webb aims to make a demanding situation feel more manageable and meaningful without pretending the difficulty is unreal. A reset creates room for a useful response; it does not remove the underlying demand.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Recenter
Use ABC: Awareness of the user’s own response, comfortable Breathing if welcome, and Centering on what matters. Notice personal Cues and Tells without diagnosing them or prescribing a breathing regimen.

### Reframe
Examine a plausible alternative interpretation supported by the facts and identify an action it makes possible. Do not insist that loss, abuse, or bad news is secretly good.

### Reward
Look for a real experience of competence or connection: a manageable contribution, a supportive conversation, or a meaningful small win. Avoid empty reassurance.

### Recharge
Identify an achievable recovery move and a boundary or workload adjustment where needed. Rest is not a prize to be earned after impossible demands are met.

### Reflect
Notice what went well and what was learned, while acknowledging what remained difficult. Identify the next priority rather than conducting an exhaustive self-critique.

## Supporting techniques and spotlights
Measured Authenticity: acknowledge the visible effect of your response, share only appropriate context, state a real plan, and apologize when warranted. Do not burden colleagues with responsibility for regulating the leader’s emotions.

## Useful output
One chosen reset, one concrete next action, and any condition that needs changing rather than merely enduring. Do not require all five resets.

## Original coaching question
What would make the next part of this situation a little more manageable, without denying what is difficult?

## Small practice
Choose one early cue that tells you to pause and one When-Then response you could use at that moment.

## Observe and adjust
The user gains enough steadiness to choose a useful action, and genuine workload or safety issues remain visible.

## Common misuse
Do not moralize stress, promise calm, diagnose mental health, or turn resilience into advice to tolerate mistreatment.
