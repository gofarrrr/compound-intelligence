# 19 · How to Respond to Failures and Mistakes

**Framework:** Voyage of Discovery

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 19, printed pp. 288–302. **Pillar:** Reasoned Resilience.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Respond to an actual mistake or failure by stabilizing, investigating, acting, and learning without confusing learning with absence of accountability.

## Route elsewhere when
For immediate safety threats, first use appropriate incident or emergency procedures. A hypothetical risk belongs in ci-decide or scenario planning.

## Coaching explanation
Webb separates a useful response to failure from reflexive blame. Reliable learning requires understanding what happened and what contributed, then changing the work rather than merely assigning a moral label.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Orient Yourself
Steady the leader’s response enough to handle the event responsibly. Address immediate harm and follow the governing incident process before a general coaching exercise.

### Rally the Crew
Acknowledge the difficulty and make it possible to surface information early. Focus the immediate effort on understanding and repair; do not promise immunity or prejudge blame.

### Map the Terrain
Involve relevant people, distinguish knowns and unknowns, and refocus on the actual problem. Investigate conditions and interactions as well as individual actions. Look at what worked without using it to dismiss the failure.

### Chart a Course
Choose practical responses using explicit criteria and options. Assign clear owners, actions, timing, and truthful updates within the user’s authority.

### Land the Learning
After stabilization, identify what can reasonably be learned, what should change, and how to check the change. Keep observations separate from causal claims and process quality separate from luck.

## Supporting techniques and spotlights
Consequences: examine system conditions, individual behavior, recurrence, and prior feedback or support before proportionate human review. Serious ethical or safety breaches may need immediate formal handling. A learning conversation does not replace those obligations.

## Useful output
A response plan ordered by urgency, an evidence-and-unknowns map, owners for corrective actions, and a bounded learning review. Lessons remain provisional until supported.

## Original coaching question
What do we know about what happened, and what are we still assuming about why it happened?

## Small practice
In the review, name one process change and one observation that would show whether it helped; do not save “this always works” from a single recovery.

## Observe and adjust
The immediate problem is addressed, the investigation remains evidence-led, and the proposed lesson has a clear scope and test.

## Common misuse
Do not erase accountability, manufacture causes, or infer that a successful recovery proves the original decision was good.
