# 05 · How to Mentally Prepare for a Key Meeting

**Framework:** Triple-A Checklist — Aim, Assumptions, Attitude

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 5, printed pp. 76–84. **Pillar:** Deliberate Wisdom.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Prepare the user’s mental stance for an important meeting, especially when tension or assumptions may interfere.

## Route elsewhere when
Use ci-communicate for message reception and ci-discuss for the design of a team discussion. A specific disagreement may need ci-disagree after preparation.

## Coaching explanation
Webb asks leaders to direct their attention before the interaction: choose a constructive aim, loosen untested assumptions, and deliberately select how to show up.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Aim
Identify the worthwhile result beyond winning, impressing, or proving someone wrong. Preserve legitimate boundaries and accountability; a constructive aim need not be agreement.

### Assumptions
Separate what is known from predictions and interpretations. Test sweeping words such as always or never. Generate plausible alternatives without declaring them true.

### Attitude
Choose a useful stance such as curiosity, steadiness, or directness, and name an observable behavior that would express it. Prepare to give the interaction adequate attention.

### When-Then
Identify a likely trigger, the response the user intends to use, and a repair move if they drift from that intention. Rehearse a brief opening when useful.

## Useful output
A compact meeting-preparation card: aim, one assumption to test, chosen attitude, opening, and one When-Then response.

## Original coaching question
What would a useful outcome look like even if the other person does not agree with you?

## Small practice
Pause before the meeting and say the three As in your own words; afterward, notice one moment when your chosen attitude changed your action.

## Observe and adjust
The preparation changes a concrete behavior, not only the user’s preferred emotional state.

## Common misuse
Do not promise to control another person’s reaction or turn a meeting into a performance of false calm.
