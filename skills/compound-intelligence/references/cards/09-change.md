# 09 · How to Change the Way People Work

**Framework:** CASE — Clear, Appealing, Social, Easy

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 9, printed pp. 136–152. **Pillar:** Intrinsic Inspiration.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Turn an intended change into observable working behavior by addressing clarity, incentives, social context, and friction.

## Route elsewhere when
Use ci-align when the goal itself is not understood. Do not diagnose noncompliance from labels such as resistant or lazy without examining the conditions.

## Coaching explanation
Webb treats behavior change as more than telling people why it matters. Instructions, incentives, norms, and practical obstacles must make the requested behavior possible and worthwhile.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Clear
Specify From-To behaviors, examples, and what success would look like in the work itself. Use a bounded pilot when helpful. Understand the purpose of an existing practice before removing it: Chesterton’s Fence.

### Appealing
Check both formal and informal rewards. Look for a mismatch between the behavior requested and the behavior actually recognized, promoted, or protected.

### Social
Use credible role models, shared routines, and real opportunities to learn from peers. Describe actual participation honestly; never invent social proof.

### Easy
Check Skills, Information, Processes, and Workload. Remove the highest-impact obstacle rather than repeating the message more loudly. Make the new behavior practical at the moment it is needed.

## Supporting techniques and spotlights
Role Modeling Change, pp. 151–152: the leader’s observable behavior must align with the change, including the small choices that signal what really matters.

## Useful output
A concrete From-To behavior and a CASE diagnosis distinguishing supported barriers from hypotheses, with one intervention and a test.

## Original coaching question
What does the present system make easier or safer than the behavior you are asking for?

## Small practice
Observe one real instance of the workflow and remove one verified friction before sending another reminder.

## Observe and adjust
The target behavior changes under ordinary conditions, not only when the leader is watching.

## Common misuse
Do not equate an announcement, training attendance, or stated agreement with adoption.
