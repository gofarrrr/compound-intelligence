# 21 · How to Build Healthy Resilience in Your Team

**Framework:** 5 Cs of Team Resilience — Conversation, Calendar, Calm, Cause, Capacity

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 21, printed pp. 319–331. **Pillar:** Reasoned Resilience.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Improve sustainable team working conditions, especially when pressure, overload, unclear roles, or inadequate recovery persist.

## Route elsewhere when
Use ci-reset for an individual’s immediate response. Do not prescribe individual mindset exercises instead of reducing structurally impossible demands.

## Coaching explanation
Webb’s team resilience includes the conditions of work, not just people’s determination. Meaning and supportive habits help, but capacity and recovery must also be real.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Conversation
Agree practical healthy-work norms, early warning signals, and ways to raise concerns. Invite discussion of relevant constraints without requiring personal disclosure or turning wellbeing into surveillance.

### Calendar
Make room for recovery, focused work, relationships, movement, and reflection where appropriate to the work. Leaders should model usable norms rather than praising a boundary and violating it themselves.

### Calm
Consider breaks, recovery after intense periods, time away, and response expectations. Choose boundaries that fit genuine operational needs and do not silently transfer all out-of-hours work to less powerful colleagues.

### Cause
Reconnect work with real purpose, beneficiaries, and pride. Do not use purpose to rationalize endless extra work or ignore a harmful environment.

### Capacity
Examine work volume, emotional demands, role clarity, skills, and resources. Stop, defer, simplify, or resource work where needed. Make explicit trade-offs rather than asking the same people to deliver everything with less.

## Useful output
A team-pressure diagnosis grounded in observed conditions, one or two changes to work, responsible owners, and an agreed way to review whether the changes help.

## Original coaching question
What needs to change in the work itself, rather than in how hard people try to cope with it?

## Small practice
Choose one demand to stop, defer, or resource; check whether the change genuinely frees capacity and does not merely move the burden.

## Observe and adjust
Working conditions and usable capacity improve, not just participation in a wellbeing activity.

## Common misuse
Do not diagnose burnout, collect health scores, mandate personal disclosures, or treat resilience as an instruction to endure harmful conditions.
