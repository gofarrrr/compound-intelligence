# 20 · How to Handle Exits and Layoffs Fairly

**Framework:** Compassionate Clarity

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 20, printed pp. 303–318. **Pillar:** Reasoned Resilience.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Support a human-led, properly authorized performance-review or exit process, or prepare factual, humane communication about a confirmed decision.

## Route elsewhere when
Do not decide who to hire, fire, retain, or rank; do not infer fitness from protected or sensitive traits. Do not invent legal requirements, notice terms, or a performance record.

## Coaching explanation
Webb combines kindness with explicit facts and expectations. Ambiguity can prolong difficulty, while clarity without care can cause avoidable harm. The framework is a process aid, not authority to make a personnel decision.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Clear Head
Clarify the actual decision and whether action and inaction have both been considered. Keep a performance concern distinct from a role elimination or layoff. The responsible humans retain the decision.

### Clear Information
Use specific, dated evidence, including relevant strengths and prior support. Mark missing facts. Never fabricate incidents, turn allegations into findings, or use irrelevant personal characteristics.

### Clear Framing
Clarify requirements, the gap, available support, and the applicable review process with the appropriate HR or other responsible function. Do not invent policy, jurisdictional rules, or a predetermined outcome disguised as a fair opportunity.

### Clear Concerns
Prepare a direct explanation of the role-related concern, supporting evidence, and what needs to change under the verified process. Acknowledge where the colleague’s perspective is still needed.

### Clear Review Process
Check that expectations, feedback, support, timing, and review ownership are real and understood. Do not label the process fair solely because a checklist has been completed.

### Clear Exit Conversation
Only after an authorized decision and process, help prepare a truthful message, verified terms, communication plan, and available support. Use appropriate human HR/legal review for consequential details.

## Supporting techniques and spotlights
Layoffs: consider alternatives and a structured process; equip managers with verified information; reduce unnecessary limbo; support affected people and reprioritize for those remaining. Do not offer false “never again” assurances. Implementation safeguard: this package will not call an exit a mutual agreement unless that is factually true, and will not prescribe blanket access-removal or employment-law rules. These are explicit package boundaries, not quotations of the book.

## Useful output
A process-readiness checklist or factual conversation scaffold, clearly stating missing approvals, facts, terms, or professional review. Never a personnel-selection verdict.

## Original coaching question
Which facts, decisions, and terms are actually confirmed, and what still needs the accountable human process?

## Small practice
Review each sentence of the planned message for factual accuracy, dignity, and promises the organization can actually keep.

## Observe and adjust
No invented fact, false implication of agreement, or unverified legal assurance remains, and the action stays with authorized people.

## Common misuse
Do not use the language of compassion to disguise coercion, discriminatory selection, or a sham review.
