# 18 · How to Help a Colleague Who’s Struggling

**Framework:** CALM — Check, Ask, Listen, Make things Manageable

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 18, printed pp. 274–287. **Pillar:** Reasoned Resilience.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Prepare a supportive conversation when a colleague appears to be having difficulty and the reasons are not yet understood.

## Route elsewhere when
This is not diagnosis, therapy, or a substitute for urgent professional help. Do not route directly to corrective feedback or dismissal on the basis of a vague sign of struggle.

## Coaching explanation
Webb starts with curiosity and listening before solutions. A colleague may need understanding and changes to the situation, not a judgment about their motivation or character.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Check
Examine assumptions and separate observed changes from explanations. Consider situational difficulty without declaring its cause or requiring personal information.

### Ask
Describe the observation and concern respectfully, then invite the colleague’s account. Make clear that they need not disclose private health or personal details to discuss work-related support.

### Listen
Acknowledge what has been said, explore the hardest part and what is still working, and resist immediate fixing. Ask what help would be useful rather than deciding for them.

### Make things Manageable
Consider changes to workload, priorities, handoffs, support, time away, or access to appropriate resources. Check effects on other colleagues so the burden is not silently displaced. Agree a proportionate, nonintrusive check-in.

## Useful output
A supportive opening, an open question, practical support options within the user’s authority, and appropriate boundaries or referral considerations.

## Original coaching question
What have you noticed, and how could you ask about it without deciding in advance what it means?

## Small practice
In the conversation, reflect back what you heard before suggesting a solution, then ask what would make the immediate work more manageable.

## Observe and adjust
The person has room to be heard and discuss workable support without being pressed to disclose sensitive information.

## Common misuse
Do not diagnose, make assurances about confidentiality that the role cannot guarantee, or record private health information in the learning home.
