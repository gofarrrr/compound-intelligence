# 10 · How to Communicate and Be Heard

**Framework:** Four Frequencies — Personal, Team, Leader, Message

**Source:** Webb, *Leadership Intelligence*, Chapter 10, printed pp. 153–166. **Pillar:** Intrinsic Inspiration.

The named framework and its central moves are source-derived. The routing boundaries, output format, original coaching question, practice check, and safety constraints are implementation adaptations, not quotations or independently validated interventions.

## Select this when
Help a leadership message be understood and received, especially when people need clarity, respect, and a reason to engage.

## Route elsewhere when
This is not a general writing, copyediting, brand-voice, or persuasion-optimization tool. Use ci-feedback for a specific developmental conversation and ci-uncertainty for crisis communication.

## Coaching explanation
Webb connects communication to what matters to the audience, their shared endeavor, the leader’s genuine commitment, and a clear message. A polished script cannot compensate for false promises or absent agency.

## Apply the relevant moves

### Define From-To
Identify the audience’s known starting point and the intended shift in understanding or action. Label assumptions about what they think or feel rather than presenting them as facts.

### Personal
Acknowledge the audience’s actual concerns and what is genuinely relevant to them. Offer only real choices, benefits, and forms of influence.

### Team
Connect the message to a shared purpose or identity that actually exists. Avoid implying consensus or belonging that the situation does not support.

### Leader
Express authentic commitment, care, and relevant experience. Never invent a personal story or emotion for the speaker.

### Message
Use a small number of clear, concrete themes and signposts. Support consequential claims with verified facts and representative examples. Reinforce the message consistently and make room to check understanding.

## Supporting techniques and spotlights
Readiness Reframe, p. 166, offers a way to interpret situational nerves before speaking. Treat it as an optional preparation move, not a treatment or a guarantee of confidence.

## Useful output
An audience-aware communication plan and, when useful, a short original opening or conversation scaffold with factual placeholders clearly marked.

## Original coaching question
What might the audience need to understand or trust before this message is useful to them?

## Small practice
Ask a listener what they understood the main point and next step to be, rather than only asking whether the message sounded good.

## Observe and adjust
The intended audience can identify the meaning and next action, and the message does not exceed the facts or promises the user can support.

## Common misuse
Do not use empathy language to disguise a fixed decision as participation or remove uncomfortable but material facts.
