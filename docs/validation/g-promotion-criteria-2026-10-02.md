## Human review procedure

Use a score-free, neutral packet containing case evidence, numbered draft sentences and selected source cards. Replace original case IDs with neutral aliases and omit `scenario_family`, authored labels, Jev outputs, question wording, thresholds and experiment descriptions. Keep the alias mapping and this criteria document with the coordinator; do not send them to reviewers before their judgments are complete.

Recruit reviewers who have not seen the results. Someone who has already read the probability tables cannot supply a score-blind judgment of this corpus; record any exposure honestly rather than relabeling it as blind. Reviewers must not compare answers before submission. The packet asks whether an unsupported asserted claim is present, which sentences and why; it separately asks about framework burden relative to the requested objective. Uncertain judgments and non-applicable framework review are allowed and retained until adjudication.

After both returns, freeze dated copies and hashes. Preserve disagreements before any adjudication. An adjudicator resolving core disagreements must also be blind to model scores and author labels. Record reviewer identities, actual exposure, rubric definitions, original labels and the resolution. Do not substitute Codex labels for this step. The software can check file bindings; it cannot verify identities or blindness.

The previous JSON label templates expose original IDs and scenario-family names. They remain unchanged for compatibility and are not the packet to share for this independent review. A new neutral Markdown packet and coordinator-only mapping were prepared outside the repository. Human judgments have not been collected, and no messages were sent to reviewers.

## Sequence and decision record

Stop authoring synthetic cases and running live experiments until the independent human labels are complete. Then freeze the missing-boundary cases and run their A/G comparison, followed by the balanced A/H study. Preserve failed results as well as successful ones.
